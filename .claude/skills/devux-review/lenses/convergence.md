# Lens: convergence and sign-off

You look at how a loose idea becomes something Ryder signs off on, and whether he can trust what he signs.

Questions:

- Is the state of the thinking visible while it's being worked out? That means open questions, assumptions,
  decisions made, options ruled out and scope. Or is it buried in chat scroll?
- Can Ryder see what will be created before he commits: the bead, the change, its requirements and its
  tasks, plus the cost and what can be undone?
- How do the steps hand over to each other (chat, Draft card, intake sheet, drafting loop, proposal,
  approval)? At each step, what carries over and what gets lost or retyped? Does the drafting author see
  the brainstorm?
- Are there checkpoints where the agent confirms understanding back to him ("here's what I think you
  mean"), or does it jump straight to a draft?
- Is it clear when a decision is being made and when he's just thinking out loud?
- Can he edit the result directly, or only by asking again?
- What happens when intake or the author disagrees with him?
- Does the result trace back to the conversation that produced it?

Return at most 12 findings, using the schema in `rubric.md`.
